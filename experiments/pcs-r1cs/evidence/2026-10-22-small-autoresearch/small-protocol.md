# Small-tree optimization protocol

The wordwise trace candidate is screened with nine alternating fresh-process pairs on eight leaves and chain length 16. Exclude one warmup per implementation. Retain only if root preparation plus proving improves by at least 3% in the paired median, at least eight of nine pairs improve, and a deterministic paired bootstrap median 95% interval excludes zero. Require exactly identical corresponding proof lengths and all circuit shape metadata.

A passing screen must replicate with seven new pairs on eight leaves and chain length 256, plus three pairs each at two and four leaves with chain length 16. Report total process time as well as root preparation plus proving. Do not claim a full-length workload improvement. Reject any regression in proof validity or geometry.

Worker-count experiments reuse one frozen baseline executable. Screen 4, 8 and 16 workers on four leaves and chain length 256 with alternating order and three repeats, then validate the best promising setting with seven new matched pairs at eight leaves and chain length 256. A runtime-setting gain is scoped to this host and workload, and is not a protocol optimization. No two timing processes run concurrently.

The stack-transposition candidate removes an overwritten serial zero-fill and initializes bounded parallel chunks directly in reserved capacity. It must pass all native tests, all-target Clippy and formatting. Screen seven alternating pairs at four leaves and chain length 256, with independent eight-leaf validation of nine pairs if the screen improves median root or whole-process time by at least 3% with at least six of seven positive pairs and bootstrap median 95% lower bound above zero. Validation requires at least eight of nine positive pairs and the same threshold. No geometry or proof-length change is permitted.

After the smaller-worker screen, test 16, 32 and 64 workers with the same affinity of CPUs 0-63 for every configuration, four leaves and chain length 256, three repeats plus an excluded warmup. This is an operating-point experiment using more CPU workers, not an algorithmic speedup. If promising, validate the winning setting against 16 workers with nine fresh alternating pairs on eight leaves and chain length 256, requiring at least 3% paired root improvement, eight of nine positive pairs and a positive bootstrap median 95% lower bound. Do not generalize across machines.

The standalone arithmetic zerocheck was only 22 ms in the diagnostic root profile, approximately 2% of root proving. Its first-message identity optimization is rejected before implementation because even eliminating that entire stage would have a low end-to-end ceiling.

The original nested recorder observes exit every 0.5 s. The wider-worker validation uses an archived copy of the same recorder with 0.01 s polling and its original checkout root fixed explicitly. Both configurations use exactly the same recorder; every receipt hashes it. Root application timers are unaffected. Whole-process comparisons between screens and validation have different exit-observation granularity; only matched pairs within one series are compared.
