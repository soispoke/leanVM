# Security of `flock::quotient`

A Lean 4 development over [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib) and VCVio for the proof system of `crates/flock/src/quotient.rs`, described in `doc/quotient/main.tex`. No `sorry`; the theorems named below depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, which the root module pins.

## What is proved

**Start with `QuotientSecurity/Setup.lean`, then `QuotientSecurity/Statement.lean`.** The first has the definitions and no proof; the second has the theorems, on that setting, with every size discharged, and says at its top what is assumed and what is not said.

- `Instance` (`Setup.lean`): a circuit, that is a Cantor basis of 22 vectors, a wiring and the weights of the public bits. The theorems hold for every one; `Instance.exists_instance`: there is one over every field of `2^192` elements, for any wiring and weights.
- `Instance.Relation`: the relation on cells indexed by their 22 bits (every row satisfies the gate, wired cells are equal, eight public words). `Instance.sat_iff` (`SetupLemmas.lean`): it is the model's `Params.Sat`. `Instance.cellsOf`: the cells a first message holds, its witness polynomial at each cell's point.
- `Instance.soundness`: for every adversary making `Q` oracle queries, the verifier accepts while the committed cells do not satisfy the relation with probability at most `(Q + 3) / 2^128`.
- `Instance.zero_knowledge`: for every event on the view, the real experiment and the simulator's differ by at most `1 / 2^128 + |U| / |H|`; `Instance.zero_knowledge_fresh`: `1 / 2^128` from an oracle that was asked nothing.
- `Instance.completeness`: the honest proof's verdict is false with probability at most `2 / 2^128 + |U| / |H|`. `Instance.honest_messages_fit`: the honest prover's two messages are within the degrees a proof may have (`Instance.honest_legal`, the degrees of the two quotients), so the honest proof is an object the soundness theorem speaks of.

These are stated with VCVio's `Pr{let x ← mx}[p x]`; the files underneath use its older `Pr[p | mx]`, and `prEvent_eq_probEvent` (`Notation.lean`) is the bridge.

The sections below are the theorems these rest on, and what the model leaves out.

**Knowledge soundness.**

- `knowledgeSoundness` (`RandomOracle.lean`): the non-interactive proof in the random-oracle model. An adversary makes at most `Q` adaptive queries and outputs a statement and a proof; the verifier asks its three challenges of the same oracle. The probability that the verifier accepts while the cells read off the proof's first message do not satisfy the relation is at most `(Q + 3)` times the largest round error. `knowledgeSoundness_bits`: at the protocol's sizes (`q = 2^192`, `N = 2^22` cells, eight words, identities of degree at most `2^27`) that is `(Q + 3) / 2^128`. It is proved from the three round bounds below and VCVio's bound on fresh bad queries (`prEvent_randomOracle_le_of_bad_queries`), not from the next theorem: ArkLib states the implication from round-by-round soundness to the Fiat-Shamir transform with `sorry`.
- `verifier_rbrKnowledgeSoundness` (`RoundByRound.lean`): the interactive protocol is round-by-round knowledge sound in ArkLib's definition (`Verifier.rbrKnowledgeSoundness`, through its worst-case form), with errors `(2 N^2 + 4 N + words) / q`, `d / q` and `3 / q` for its three challenges, each at most `2^-128` at those sizes (`rbrError_le`).

**Completeness, of the algebra.** `Params.honest_consistent`, `Params.accepts_of_consistent` (`Completeness.lean`): given a first message whose cells satisfy the relation, the honest second message (the interpolation of the sum's values and the two quotients) is consistent with it wherever no denominator vanishes on a cell, and the four claims are then true at every challenge the verifier does not reject. These are two lemmas, not ArkLib's `completeness`; the probability statement is `completeness_prob` below, for the honest prover of the zero knowledge experiment, whose messages are taken within the degree bounds as a hypothesis. `Params.exists_witness` (`Witness.lean`): every assignment of the cells is the cells of some first message, over the one field of `Params`. It gives no degree bound on that message, and no message with coefficients in the small field, which the zero-knowledge theorems take as a hypothesis.

**Zero knowledge.**

- `zeroKnowledge` (`Distance.lean`): the non-interactive proof, in the random-oracle model with a simulator that programs the oracle. The oracle may already hold answers: the theorem is for each state `c₀`, each statement and each witness, so it covers whatever anyone asked before, and, by averaging over the state (not stated), a statement and a witness chosen from it. The real experiment draws masks and two handles, runs the honest prover against the oracle in that state, and outputs the proof, its verdict, its challenges and the oracle's new state. The simulator `simNI` takes the statement and the oracle's state only: it draws handles, challenges and four values (uniform in the cubic extension `E`, or in `K = GF(2^64)` when the challenge's row is in `K`), takes the verifier's own nonzero check for the verdict, and writes its challenges in the oracle. For every event on the outcome, the two experiments' probabilities differ by at most `zkBound = (2 N + 4 deg) / q + |U| / |H|`: `N` cells, a vanishing polynomial of degree `deg`, `q` field elements, `U` any set containing the handles the oracle was already asked about with this statement, `H` the handles; `exists_handles`: a state holding `Q` answers has such a `U` of at most `Q` handles (that `Q` queries leave at most `Q` answers is not stated). `zkError_le`: the first term is at most `2^-128` at the protocol's sizes (by hand it is about `2^-168`); the second is the chance a fresh commitment collides with an earlier query.
- Under it (`NonInteractive.lean`, `Simulation.lean`, `ZeroKnowledge.lean`): `realNI_eq_simNI`, the two experiments give exactly the same probability to every outcome whose first handle is fresh and whose challenges are good for the witness; `card_not_good_le`, few challenges are not good; `realView_eq_simView`, for fixed good challenges the honest verifier's whole view of the interactive proof has the simulator's distribution; `realValues_eq_simValues`, the four opened values are distributed as the simulator's wherever the vanishing polynomial is not zero, because three mask coefficients over `K` cover exactly one value in `E` (`maskedValue_bijective`), and one in `K`.

**What the relation means for a circuit.** `AdderCircuit.determined` (`Circuit.lean`): for a circuit of full adders with no loop (a row is an input, reading its own output and two zeros, or reads constants and rows of lower rank), two assignments of the rows' outputs, in any field of characteristic 2, that satisfy every gate and agree on the inputs agree everywhere, and every output is an element of `GF(4)`; an input row holds 0 or 1 (`input_is_bit`). So a satisfying assignment is the circuit's computation on some input bits: uniqueness is the theorem, and that the computation is a satisfying assignment is what the Rust tests check. With it, `copy_iterate` (a value is the same along a cycle of the wiring) and `word_pins` (a public word all of whose cells are one cell pins it, which is how word 5 fixes the zero; the one is then fixed by word 4, whose other cells are zeros, a step not stated in Lean).

**The parameters exist.** `Params.ofData`, `Params.ofData_exact` (`Instance.lean`): from rows whose cells are distinct points, any permutation of the cells and any public words, the polynomials the theorems assume exist, with exact vanishing polynomials. So `Params` and `Params.Exact` are not vacuous. The hypothesis of the zero knowledge theorems not exhibited in Lean is a satisfying witness over `K`.

**The instance on a Cantor basis** (`Concrete.lean`). `Params.ofCantor`: for a Cantor basis of at least `n + 2` vectors, any permutation of the `2^(n+2)` cells, any public words and any public weight that carries them, the parameters whose cells are the span of the first `n + 2` vectors, with `θ` the second vector, the cells' vanishing polynomial the digit `w_{n+2}` and the wiring polynomial the one a table holds (`CantorBasis.exists_table`: every function on the cells is a table's polynomial; `CantorBasis.point_injective`: distinct bits are distinct points). `Params.ofCantor_exact`: its vanishing polynomials are the exact ones. `claims_iff_accepts_ofCantor`: `claims_iff_accepts` on it, with no hypothesis left on the cells, the vanishing polynomial or the wiring polynomial. `knowledgeSoundness_ofCantor`: the bound `(Q + 3) / 2^128` at `n = 20` and eight public words over a field of `2^192` elements, the one size left as a hypothesis being the public weight's degree, at most `2^22 + 9 * 2^10` (`Params.ofCantorBounds` bounds every other polynomial by its table's size, `Params.ofCantor_identity`). `exists_pub`, `exists_instance`: every field of `2^192` elements has such a basis, and every choice of public words a public weight within that degree. `claims_iff_accepts_ofCantor` is for any wiring table whose polynomial carries the wiring (`CantorBasis.tablePoly_ext`: a table's polynomial is determined by its values on the cells). `rowsPoly_eq_vRows`: a sum of digits with constant coefficients, the last one, that vanishes on the rows' basis vectors is the instance's rows' polynomial; that is the linear system `row_vanishing` solves, and the Rust test `transform_matches_the_basis` checks its solution vanishes, at a small size.

**Tables as polynomials** (`Encoding.lean`, `Cantor.lean`). For a Cantor basis of `n` vectors (`beta 0 = 1` and `S(beta (k+1)) = beta k` below `n`, `S(x) = x^2 + x`; a finite field has one only up to the largest power of two dividing its degree, 64 for `GF(2^64)`; `exists_cantorBasis` (`CantorExists.lean`) proves one exists, of every length up to 64, in any field of `2^64` elements, and `exists_cantorBasis_big` in any field of `2^192` elements, which contains one of `2^64` (`CantorBasis.map` carries a basis along a ring homomorphism); the Rust test `transform_matches_the_basis` checks that the basis the prover uses is one) and tables of at most `n` variables, with a cell's point the sum of the basis vectors of its bits and digit `k` the `k`-fold `S`:

- `CantorBasis.digit_eq`: digit `k` of a cell is its bit `k` plus a function of its higher bits.
- `mle_eq_tablePoly_eval`: a multilinear claim on a table at the point `u_k = w_k(t) / (1 + w_k(t))` is the value at `t` of the table's polynomial (`tablePoly`, its entries on the products of digits) over the product of the `1 + w_k(t)`.
- `sum_tablePoly`: the claim at the point of all ones is the sum of the table's polynomial over the cells.
- `mle_snoc`: one more coordinate `e` of a table gives `(1 + e)` times its first half's claim plus `e` times its second's, which is the rule every claim's extra coordinates use.

**The four claims are the model's acceptance** (`Tables.lean`, `Assembly.lean`). `claims_iff_accepts`: let the cells be the points of a Cantor basis, the cells' vanishing polynomial its digit `w_m`, and the wiring polynomial the wiring table's. For a witness table of `m + 1` variables, a wiring table of `m` and a quotient table of `m + 6`, with entries in the large field (which covers tables over the small one), at a challenge where no `1 + w_k(t)` vanishes and the verifier's product is not zero, the four claims the verifier outputs (each table's multilinear extension at the verifier's point, equal to the verifier's value) hold exactly when `Params.Accepts` holds of the polynomials the tables hold (`witnessOfTable`, `quotientsOfTable`). Under it, one theorem per claim: `witness_claim`, `wiring_claim`, `quotient_mle`, `sum_mle`. `tables_within_bounds`: those polynomials are within the degree bounds the soundness theorems quantify over, when the bounds are at least the sizes of the polynomials the tables hold, one column of the witness table and one limb of a slot of the quotient table (`natDegree_tablePoly_le`: a table of `m` variables holds a polynomial of degree at most `2^m`).

**The verifier's own polynomials** (`PublicWeight.lean`). `coordPoly_point`: the combination of digits the verifier calls coordinate `k` is bit `k` of a cell of the public region, for constants `b` with `b 0 = 1` and `sum_e b e * beta (d - e) = 0`, the recurrence `quotient::domain::coordinates` computes. `hiPoly_point`: the product of the `1 + w_k` past the region is one on the region and zero on the other cells. `pubProd_point`, `membershipPub_hPub`: so the product the verifier evaluates is a public weight for eight words, word `J` the sum over the cells of the region with bits 8 to 10 equal to `J` of the product of the constant weights of their set bits below 8; `natDegree_membershipPub_le`: its degree is at most `2^22 + 9 * 2^10`. `knowledgeSoundness_membership`: the bound `(Q + 3) / 2^128` on `membershipParams`, the instance with these words and this weight, over any field of `2^192` elements, any Cantor basis and any wiring; `membership_target`: its target on the statement's four words followed by `(1, 0, 0, 0)` is the verifier's. The theorem is for any constants `c` and any `b` with that recurrence: nothing in Lean fixes them to the Rust values. What stays outside Lean here: that the Rust constants are these (the basis, `b` from `coordinates`, and `c` the bit weights, a cell's weight being the product over its set bits, which the Rust test `public_weights_are_products_over_coordinates` checks), that the product in `reduce` is `pubProd`, by reading, and that the wiring is the circuit's.

**Zero knowledge and completeness on the instance** (`ConcreteZK.lean`, `CompletenessProb.lean`). `exists_subfield`: a field of `2^192` elements is of degree 3 over one of `2^64`. For a Cantor basis in the small field, carried to the large one, `zeroKnowledge_ofCantor` is `zeroKnowledge` on the instance with the mask's vanishing polynomial the digit `w_n` (`ofCantor_van`: it vanishes where the theorem asks), and `zkError_ofCantor` its `2^-128` at `n = 20`. `completeness_prob`: from any oracle state, the honest prover's proof gets a false verdict with probability at most the zero knowledge bound plus `identity / q`, the fraction of points where the product the verifier inverts is zero (`rejectPoly`, nonzero because a denominator is nonzero on a cell). `completeness_ofCantor`: on the instance at `n = 20`, at most `2 / 2^128` plus the fraction of handles the oracle was asked about. The verdict is the model's: the Rust verifier also rejects the `t` with some `1 + w_k(t) = 0`, a further `2^24 / q`, not counted here.

## The model, and what it leaves out

- **The protocol is stated on polynomials** (`Protocol.lean`). A table is read as the polynomials it holds, a claim on a table as an evaluation of them, and the claim at the fixed point as the sum of the low part of the sum's polynomial over the cells. `claims_iff_accepts` is the link from the claims on tables to this. Its points, scales and values are those of the Rust `reduce` and its tables' layout that of `prove`, compared by a review coordinate by coordinate, since the model's tables are indexed by their bits, with no integer index to compare with Rust's. The theorem is for any `Params` whose cells' vanishing polynomial, wiring polynomial and cells are the basis's: on `membershipParams` the rows' polynomial, the public weight and the target are the verifier's (`rowsPoly_eq_vRows`, `membershipPub_hPub`, `membership_target`), up to the constants' values; `Params.ofCantor` is a `Params` with these properties.
- **`Params.Accepts` against the Rust verifier.** An independent review compared it with `reduce` formula by formula and found no mismatch. The model drops the scale factors of the claims, which are nonzero constants under the verifier's own check, and it does not reject a challenge with `1 + w_k(t) = 0`: it accepts more, which is safe for soundness, and differs from the real verdict on a public fraction of about `2^24 / q` of the challenges for completeness and zero knowledge. The Rust `verify` returns the four claims; "accepts" here means they are true of the committed polynomials.
- **Commitments are ideal.** For soundness the committed object is the polynomials themselves, of bounded degree, and they are what the hash absorbs: this is Fiat-Shamir soundness of the polynomial protocol, to which a real commitment adds its binding and extraction error and the hash chain its collisions. The extractor reads the cells off the proof, which in this model contains the polynomials; nothing is extracted from a commitment. For zero knowledge a commitment is a uniformly random handle and the verifier learns of the tables only whether its four claims are true, as one bit with its own check: the hiding of real commitments and openings is assumed. A real one needs the tables padded or masked, the quotient table included, which is not built.
- **The hash is a random oracle** on what the transcript has absorbed, one oracle with three kinds of input, so the chain cannot collide.
- **The oracle is this protocol's only**: other uses of the same hash are outside the model.
- **The adversary** of `knowledgeSoundness` is a computation over the oracle only, with no coins of its own; `soundness_after` gives the bound for one with coins, in the handle-keyed game.
- **Zero knowledge for several proofs** is `zeroKnowledge_seq` (`Sequence.lean`): the statements and witnesses are fixed in advance, each proof is made from the oracle state the last one left, and every event on the whole sequence of outcomes and tables has probabilities within the sum of the single bounds, the `i`-th with `q + 3 i` hash inputs in the table. Statements chosen by an adversary after seeing earlier proofs are not covered, nor oracle queries made between two proofs, which would enter through the count of hash inputs. What a distinguisher asks after the proof is a function of the outcome, which carries the oracle's whole state; a distinguisher is not a formal object, and the bound is two inequalities for every event, which is a bound on the total variation distance, not stated as one. The witness is over `K`, with `E` any extension of degree 3. `Good`, the set of challenges at which the simulation is exact, depends on the witness. On a challenge the verifier rejects the Rust prover panics, and on one where a denominator vanishes on a cell it outputs a proof whose claims are false; the Lean experiment outputs a proof with a false verdict in both cases, all of them charged to the bound. The simulator is not shown to be efficient.
- **Soundness with the zero knowledge theorem's oracle, and after simulated proofs** (`Joint.lean`). This is not zero knowledge and soundness of one scheme in one game: no theorem here relates real and simulated proofs whose handles are the commitments of their messages. One oracle, keyed by the statement and the commitments' handles as in the zero knowledge theorem, and binding commitments (the handle determines the message, two injective maps). `knowledgeSoundness_handles`: the soundness bound for any adversary over that oracle, which may ask about handles that commit to nothing. `soundness_after`: the same after any computation over the oracle with its own coins, its queries counted; this is also the bound for an adversary with coins. `simRun_eq_simNI`: the zero knowledge simulator need not program, since asking the oracle for its challenges gives the same outcome and table at every outcome whose first handle is fresh. `simulationSoundness`: after one simulated proof of a statement fixed in advance, `(Q + 6)` times the round error; it is `soundness_after` at the simulator's three queries, which an adversary could make itself, and a statement chosen after oracle queries is the general `soundness_after`, not spelled out. Left out: the commitment is binding and not hiding in this game (the simulator's handles are uniform, a message's handle is a function of it), several simulated proofs are the general `soundness_after` and not spelled out, and a simulated proof replayed by the adversary is not a proof here, its handles committing to nothing the verifier can check.
- **The relation** is `Params.Sat`: the cells, elements of the large field, satisfy every gate, every copy constraint and the public words. Left to the circuit and not proved in Lean: that the wiring of `quotient::circuit` is the permutation of the instance; that the Rust circuit is an `AdderCircuit` wired as `Circuit.lean` assumes, with its zero and one anchored in words 5 and 4: this is not proved in Lean, but the Rust test `membership_is_a_wired_loop_free_circuit` checks every hypothesis of `AdderCircuit` and `Wired` on the real circuit (input rows read their own output and two zeros; every other row reads constants or rows of lower rank, the public rows ranked last and read by none; every reading cell is on the cycle of the output cell it reads; the constants' cycles hold their anchors and no output; the statement's public cells read rows that drop the carry); that words 0 to 3 determine the statement's bits, their cells being wired to rows that drop the carry and their weights independent over GF(2); and that this circuit's computation is the BLAKE2s statement, which a Rust test checks on a random input. `cells_determined` puts `AdderCircuit.determined` on the protocol's cells: if every reading cell is taken by some power of the wiring to the output cell it reads (or to the cell holding the constant), cells satisfying the gates and the copies, with the zero and the one pinned, have outputs determined by the input rows. `Params` also has degenerate instances (no rows, no words), where the theorems hold and say little.
- **Soundness needs only polynomials that vanish** on the cells and the rows; completeness and zero knowledge need them exact (`Params.Exact`), and zero knowledge needs the mask's vanishing polynomial `van` nonzero and zero on the rows. `van` is in the row variable (in the protocol `w_20`, of degree `2^20`), not `Params.vRows`, which is in the cell variable.

## Structured R1CS comparison

`QuotientSecurity/R1CS.lean` formalizes the six rank-one equations used for each full-adder row by `crates/rec_aggregation/examples/comparison_support/r1cs.rs`. It proves that the first five equations uniquely determine the auxiliary wires, and that eliminating them is equivalent in both directions to the original quotient gate. The full relation retains the copy wiring and eight packed public word equations. `Instance.r1cs_soundness` transports `Instance.soundness` through this exact equivalence: the probability of accepting while the committed cells and their canonical auxiliary extension fail that R1CS relation is at most `(Q + 3) / 2^128` under precisely the upstream ideal binding commitment and random-oracle model. The auxiliary wires are extracted from the committed cells; they are not additional commitments.

This is a proof for the structured full-adder R1CS format and fixed upstream dimensions. It is not arbitrary sparse R1CS support, nor a proof of Rust refinement, concrete PCS binding, the recursive VM verifier, Keccak gadget, circuit registration or application identity hashing. As in the original theorem, the wiring and public weights are parameters: the theorem does not establish that their concrete Rust values implement BLAKE2s membership. In particular, the formal relation uses eight weighted public word equations, whereas the diagnostic Rust checker checks 1,024 individual public bits. Identifying those relations for the concrete encoding additionally requires its bitness and injective packing argument; that concrete identification is not claimed here. The full `lake build` passed on 2026-10-20, including all seven new axiom guards and the existing formalization. The [validation record](../../experiments/pcs-r1cs/evidence/2026-10-20-formal/README.md) preserves source hashes and the build log.
